Fixture AI
Prediction Methodology
Overview
Opponent-Strength Engine

Most prediction models look at raw form — how many wins a team has had recently. The problem: a team that has beaten five bottom-half sides in a row looks identical to a team that drew with the league leader and lost narrowly to the top four. Fixture AI fixes this by weighting every result by the quality of the opponent faced.

The core idea is that beating a #1-ranked team is worth far more than beating a #18-ranked team, and drawing against a top-4 side can be more valuable than three wins against relegation candidates. The algorithm identifies and penalises "false form" — a hot streak built entirely on weak opposition.

Algorithm
Step-by-step walkthrough
1
Fetch recent match history
For each team in the fixture, the last 10 finished matches are pulled from the API. Every match includes the final score and which team was home or away. The opponent's current league position is looked up from the standings table to determine their quality.
2
Score opponent difficulty (SoS)
Each opponent is assigned a Strength of Schedule (SoS) score between 0 and 1. The league leader scores 1.0, the bottom side scores 0.0, everyone else scales linearly in between. This means a win against #1 carries maximum weight, while a win against #20 carries the minimum.

Formula: SoS = 1 − (oppRank − 1) / (totalTeams − 1)
3
Apply recency and difficulty weighting
Each match gets a compound weight combining two factors:

• Recency factor — the most recent match is weighted 1.0, the oldest match is weighted 0.5, with linear decay in between. Recent form matters more.
• Difficulty factor — ranges from 0.4 (weakest opponent) to 1.0 (strongest opponent). A result against a top side counts up to 2.5× more than the same result against a bottom side.

difficulty = 0.4 + SoS × 0.6
weight = recencyFactor × difficulty
4
Calculate per-match performance score
Each match is converted to a raw performance value before being weighted. The base is the result (win = 100, draw = 33, loss = 0), then adjusted by:

• Goal difference bonus — scaled up by opponent strength. A 3-0 win against a strong side adds much more than a 3-0 win against a weak side.
• Clean sheet bonus — also scaled by opponent strength (keeping a clean sheet against #1 matters more).
• Heavy concession penalty — conceding 3 or more goals deducts points regardless of the result.
5
Aggregate into a team score (0–100)
All weighted performance values are averaged into a single opponent-adjusted team score. A small league-position bonus (up to 18 points) is added on top so current standing is still considered — but recent form always dominates. The final score is clamped between 2 and 98 so no team is rated as completely unbeatable or hopeless.
6
Convert scores to win/draw/away probabilities
The home team receives a fixed +8 point home advantage bonus. Both scores are then converted to shares of the total (so a 60 vs 40 gap becomes 60% home vs 40% away before draw is applied).

Draw probability is calculated separately — it rises when two teams are closely matched and shrinks as the gap widens. This is what allows draws and away wins to appear naturally rather than having home win always dominate:

P(draw) = 0.32 − |scoreGap| × 0.006 [clamped 14%–34%]

The remaining probability is split between home and away proportionally, then all three are normalised to sum to exactly 100%.

Key concepts
What this model rewards and penalises
🏆
Beating strong opponents
A win against the #1-ranked team is weighted at maximum difficulty (1.0). The same result against a bottom-half team is weighted at roughly 0.4 — more than 2× less valuable.
🤝
Drawing against top sides
A draw against a league leader can outweigh three wins against relegation candidates once difficulty weighting is applied. The model sees the quality of opposition, not just the result.
⚠️
False form detection
A team with 5 wins in a row against bottom-half sides will score lower than a team with 2 wins and 2 draws against top-half sides. Easy fixtures don't translate to high scores.
📅
Recency decay
Results from 10 matches ago count half as much as last week's result. Teams are assessed on their current trajectory, not what they did at the start of the season.
⚽
Margin of victory matters
A 4-0 win is worth more than a 1-0 win, especially against strong opponents. Goal difference bonuses scale with the difficulty of the opponent faced.
🏠
Home advantage
Every home team receives a fixed +8 point bonus before probabilities are calculated. This reflects the well-documented statistical advantage of playing at home.

Limitations
What the model doesn't know
Injuries and suspensions — the API doesn't provide squad availability data on the free tier, so a team missing its first-choice goalkeeper or striker will still be rated on its historical results.

Early season — when fewer than 5 finished matches are available, the model falls back to league position as the primary signal. Predictions are less reliable in the first few gameweeks.

Cup and international matches — the recent-form lookup pulls all finished matches, which may include domestic cups or continental games against non-league opponents whose ranking is unknown. These are assigned a mid-table difficulty estimate.

Manager changes, motivation, and context — a team with nothing to play for in the final gameweek, or a manager sacked mid-week, will not be reflected in the model. Use predictions as one input alongside your own knowledge.
